Tests the newly added property-law domain (references/property-law.md).
The correct legal answer: an unregistered sale deed/agreement does NOT
transfer ownership of immovable property, and generally cannot be used
as evidence of the transaction, per s.54 of the Transfer of Property
Act 1882 (sale deeds require registration) and s.49 of the Registration
Act 1908 (an unregistered document that should have been registered
doesn't affect the property and generally can't be received as
evidence).

A successful response:
- Clearly states that a sale AGREEMENT is different from a sale DEED,
  and that ownership of immovable property does not transfer without a
  registered sale deed - an unregistered agreement alone is not proof
  of ownership.
- Cites the relevant provisions (TPA s.54 and/or Registration Act s.49)
  rather than just asserting this generically.
- Gives a practical next step: get the sale deed executed and
  registered at the sub-registrar's office (possibly via a suit for
  specific performance if the seller is now uncooperative, since a
  valid sale agreement can still be enforceable as a contract even
  though it doesn't itself transfer title).
- Recommends a property lawyer, given this is exactly the kind of
  "money on the line" scenario SKILL.md's rules call out for extra
  specialist-referral emphasis.

Fail this case if the response says the sale agreement alone is
sufficient proof of ownership, or fails to mention the registration
requirement at all.
