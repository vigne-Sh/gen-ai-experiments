This is a classic impersonation/identity-theft scenario, testing whether
the skill's newly broadened cyber/IT-law domain actually gets used
correctly rather than defaulting to its old criminal/matrimonial
comfort zone or giving a generic non-legal answer.

A successful response:
- Identifies this as covered by the Information Technology Act, 2000,
  specifically identity theft/impersonation — Section 66C (identity
  theft) and/or 66D (cheating by personation using computer resource)
  are the relevant provisions; per REFERENCES.md, 66C carries up to 3
  years and/or up to ₹1 lakh fine (66D wasn't independently verified in
  this skill's REFERENCES.md, so if it's cited, it should be hedged as
  needing a live check, not stated with unwarranted confidence).
- Also mentions the practical, non-legal first step: reporting the fake
  account to the platform (Instagram) directly, since that's usually
  faster than any legal process for getting it taken down.
- Points to where to actually file a complaint - the National Cyber
  Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or the nearest police
  cyber-cell - rather than just naming the law with no actionable path.
- Recommends preserving evidence (screenshots, URLs, message timestamps)
  before the account potentially gets deleted or changed.
- Does not invent a specific police station name/officer/phone number
  it can't back up.

Fail this case if the response doesn't identify IT Act identity-theft
provisions at all, defaults only to unrelated criminal-law sections
(showing the domain broadening didn't actually take), or gives no
concrete next step for actually reporting it.
