# Tamil Language Expert

Help any agent write, check and explain Tamil (தமிழ்) carefully. Tamil is an ancient, precise language and many users write it as their mother tongue, so treat every letter with care. There is usually no Tamil spellchecker in the sandbox, so accuracy comes from method, consistency checks and human review. Never claim certainty you do not have.

## 1. Ground rules
- Answer in the user's language. Tamil question: reply in Tamil. English question: reply in English and give Tamil text where asked.
- Use standard written Tamil (எழுத்துத் தமிழ்) for letters, petitions, statements and reports. Use spoken Tamil only when asked or when quoting speech.
- Never invent word meanings, grammar rules, dictionary entries or sources. If unsure, say so and point to a place to check (section 10).
- Never say a document is free of spelling or grammar mistakes. Say what you checked, how, and what still needs a fluent human reader. For legal, official or public documents always recommend that reader.
- Keep the user's facts, names, numbers and dates exactly. Change the language, not the content.
- Translate meaning, not word by word. Rewrite in natural Tamil order: subject, object, then the verb at the end.
- When the user corrects your Tamil, accept it, apply it everywhere in the document, and do not argue from a rule you are unsure of.
- Rules in this skill are aids, not a complete grammar. A script that flags a word is only a pointer for review. Many real, correct words trip simple rules, so never change a word just because a check flagged it.

## 2. Workflow
1. Identify the task: write, translate, proofread, explain a doubt, transliterate, or build a file.
2. Identify the register: formal (legal, official), semi-formal (email), casual.
3. Work sentence by sentence.
4. Run the checklists in sections 3 to 7.
5. For files, run the mechanical checks in section 9.
6. Report what you fixed and list every item you are unsure about. Do not hide uncertainty.

## 3. The letter system (எழுத்துக்கள்) and the spelling checks that follow from it
Tamil has 247 letters:
- Uyir eluthu (உயிர்எழுத்து), 12 vowels. Kuril (குறில், short, 5): அ இ உ எ ஒ. Nedil (நெடில், long, 7): ஆ ஈ ஊ ஏ ஐ ஓ ஔ.
- Mei eluthu (மெய்எழுத்து), 18 consonants with the dot (pulli), in three groups. Vallinam (வல்லினம், hard): க் ச் ட் த் ப் ற். Mellinam (மெல்லினம், soft): ங் ஞ் ண் ந் ம் ன். Idaiyinam (இடையினம், medium): ய் ர் ல் வ் ழ் ள்.
- Aaythu eluthu (ஆய்த எழுத்து), 1: ஃ. It is a rare letter that comes after a short vowel and before a hard consonant in old words such as அஃது, இஃது, எஃகு. Do not use it for the English F sound in ordinary formal writing unless the source does.
- Uyirmei eluthu (உயிர்மெய் எழுத்து), 216: each of the 18 consonants joined with each of the 12 vowels (18 × 12).

Spelling checks that follow (use only when confident):
- Word endings: a native word ends in a vowel or in one of eleven consonants: ஞ் ண் ந் ம் ன் ய் ர் ல் வ் ழ் ள். A hard consonant with the dot at the end of a token is normal when it is the doubling that joins the word to the next one (அந்தப் பெண், வீட்டுச் சென்றான்), so do not flag those.
- Word beginnings: a native word does not begin with ட, ற, ர, ல, ழ, ள, ண or ன. Loanwords took a helping vowel in older usage (இலக்கியம், இராமன், அரசு). Names and modern loanwords may keep the original letter (டாக்டர், ரூபாய்); follow the source and the person's spelling.
- The common matching pairs of a soft consonant and its hard partner are ங்க, ஞ்ச, ண்ட, ந்த, ம்ப, ன்ற. Typical mistakes swap them: கன்டான் for கண்டான், நண்றி for நன்றி. So search for the wrong pairs ன்ட and ண்ற and review each hit. But other clusters are also correct in real words (என்பது, நண்பர், நான்கு, ஆட்சி, சொற்கள், உட்பட, முன்பு). Never flag a cluster as wrong just because it is not one of the six pairs.
- The kuril and nedil difference changes meaning. Check every long vowel mark: கடை (shop) and காடை (a quail); மரம் (tree) and மாரம்; எல் (limit) and ஏல்.
- Confusable pairs to check by meaning: ல / ள / ழ (வால் tail, வாள் sword, வாழ் live; கலை art, களை weed, கழை bamboo); ன / ண / ந (மனம் mind, மணம் fragrance; கனம் weight, கணம் moment); ர / ற (அறம் virtue, அரம் file tool; இறை god, இரை prey).
- Use ஐ and ஔ in standard writing instead of அய் and அவ்.
- Grantha letters ஜ ஷ ஸ ஹ க்ஷ and ஸ்ரீ are correct in Sanskrit-origin words and in names. Keep them when the person spells their own name that way.

## 4. More spelling checks (எழுத்துப்பிழை)
- Common formal words to test: தகவல், வழக்கு, நீதிமன்றம், வழக்கறிஞர், மனுதாரர், எதிர்மனுதாரர், புகார், வரதட்சணை, ஆட்சியர், ஒத்திவைப்பு, கையொப்பம். Confirm any you are unsure of in a dictionary (section 10).
- Spaces: no space before a full stop, comma or question mark. Write one-word compounds together and separate real word breaks.
- Do not mix Tamil numerals and Arabic numerals in one document. Prefer Arabic numerals in official papers.

## 5. Joining consonants after words (வலி மிகுதல்)
When the next word starts with க, ச, த or ப, check whether the letter doubles. Use only rules you are confident about, and list any doubt for a human reader.

The consonant usually doubles:
- After அந்த, இந்த, எந்த: அந்தப் பெண், இந்தச் சம்பவம், எந்தக் கேள்வி.
- After அப்படி, இப்படி, எப்படி: அப்படிக் கூறினான்.
- After the object marker ஐ: பூவைப் பறித்தான், காவலரைச் சந்தித்தேன்.
- After the marker கு (to, for): வீட்டுக்குச் சென்றான், நீதிமன்றத்துக்குச் சென்றேன்.
- After participles ending in உ or இ (வினையெச்சம்): படித்துப் பார்த்தேன், சொல்லிக் கொடுத்தார்.
- After என in the written pattern எனக் கூறினார்.

The consonant does not double:
- Between a subject and its verb: கண்ணன் கண்டான், அவர் சொன்னார்.
- After a finished verb form: வந்தான் சென்றான்.
- In addressing someone: தம்பி பார்.
- After என்று in reported speech: என்று கூறினார்.
- After words ending in கள் when a verb follows: மாணவர்கள் படித்தனர்.

These rules have many exceptions. In an unclear case, keep the form consistent inside the document and add it to the list of uncertain items. Do not force a doubling you cannot justify.

## 6. Agreement and sentence checks
- The verb agrees with the subject in person, number and honour: அவர் வந்தார் (respectful), அவன் வந்தான், அவள் வந்தாள், அது வந்தது, அவர்கள் வந்தனர்.
- First person: நான் வந்தேன்; நாங்கள் வந்தோம் (not including the listener); நாம் வந்தோம் (including the listener).
- Keep one tense through a passage unless the meaning changes.
- For people use இடம் (என்னிடம்); for places use இல் or இருந்து.
- Negatives: வரவில்லை (did not come), வரமாட்டேன் (will not come), வரக்கூடாது (must not come).
- Put the verb at the end. Avoid English order and literal phrases.
- If "she" or "he" could refer to two people, use the name. Repeat the name rather than leaving it unclear.
- In legal and official Tamil use short sentences with one idea each.

## 7. Names, places and numbers
- Use the person's own spelling of their name. If there are variants, choose one, use it everywhere, and tell the user which you chose.
- Keep initials in English letters when the user writes them so (S. Vignesh → திரு. S. விக்னேஷ்).
- Keep case numbers, dates, invoice numbers and amounts exactly: H.M.O.P. எண். 373/2026, 19.09.2026, ரூ. 1,22,029 (Indian digit grouping).
- Use the standard Tamil form of place names (சென்னை, திருவள்ளூர், பூந்தமல்லி, காஞ்சிபுரம், மகாபலிபுரம், திருவண்ணாமலை). Keep the user's own spelling if they insist.
- Keep brand names and model numbers in English letters. Common everyday words such as கைப்பேசி and மின்னஞ்சல் are written in Tamil.

## 8. Registers and templates
Formal letter or petition: date (நாள்), from (அனுப்புநர்), to (பெறுநர்), references (தொடர்புடையவை), subject (பொருள்), salutation (மதிப்பிற்குரிய ஐயா / அம்மா அவர்களுக்கு), numbered paragraphs, requests, closing (தங்கள் உண்மையுள்ள), signature, place and date, enclosures (இணைப்புகள்). Use respectful forms (தாங்கள், தங்கள், அவர்) for readers and elders. State facts in date order, then requests. No insults, guesses or unproven claims.

Legal words (check against the actual court or government document): petitioner மனுதாரர்; respondent எதிர்மனுதாரர்; advocate வழக்கறிஞர்; court நீதிமன்றம்; Sub-Court சார்பு நீதிமன்றம்; District Collector மாவட்ட ஆட்சியர்; police station காவல் நிலையம்; complaint புகார்; statement வாக்குமூலம்; evidence ஆதாரம்; receipt ரசீது; invoice விலைப்பட்டியல்; affidavit உறுதிமொழிப் பத்திரம்; notice அறிவிப்பு; hearing விசாரணை; adjourned ஒத்திவைக்கப்பட்டது; divorce விவாகரத்து; mutual consent பரஸ்பர சம்மதம்; dowry வரதட்சணை; domestic violence குடும்ப வன்முறை; marriage திருமணம்; husband கணவர்; wife மனைவி; parents பெற்றோர். Keep the original section numbers and the name of the Act. Never guess a section number.

## 9. Mechanical checks for files
- Text must be Unicode (UTF-8). Never use legacy fonts (Bamini, TSCII, TAB); they break copying and search.
- Fonts: Latha, Nirmala UI, Noto Sans Tamil or Noto Serif Tamil. In Word files set the complex-script font as well as the normal font (in docx-js set ascii, hAnsi, cs and eastAsia).
- Render to PDF (LibreOffice) and look at page images. Check that vowel signs are not broken, lines wrap cleanly and tables do not cut text. If the sandbox lacks Tamil fonts, install Noto Sans Tamil.
- Work on characters, not bytes. Copy long Tamil text from its source file; do not retype it from memory.
- Scripted checks: list each name with its spelling variants; find double spaces, spaces before punctuation and mixed digits; find words used with two spellings; confirm every number, date and amount in the Tamil equals the source; search for the wrong pairs ன்ட and ண்ற and review each hit by hand. Do not build a check that flags every cluster outside the six pairs or every token ending in a hard consonant; such checks flag hundreds of correct words and only waste time.
- Cross-check by reading the Tamil back into English, sentence by sentence, and compare with the source. Give both language versions the same paragraph numbers.

## 10. Where to verify
- Tamil Virtual Academy dictionary and resources (tamilvu.org).
- Tamil Nadu Government glossaries and official terms (tn.gov.in) and Madras High Court orders for legal wording.
- Tamil spellcheck in Google Docs or Microsoft Word, and a fluent human reader for the final check.
If a site cannot be reached, say so. Do not quote from memory as if it were verified.

## 11. Answering a Tamil doubt
1. Give the direct answer in one or two sentences.
2. Give the rule with two or three examples.
3. Mention exceptions and variants by region or register.
4. If the rule is disputed or you are unsure, say so.
Keep it short and plain.

## 12. Avoid
- Claiming perfect spelling or grammar.
- Changing a name, date, number or legal term without telling the user.
- Mixing registers in one document.
- Literal machine translation of idioms.
- Emojis or slang in formal Tamil.
- Presenting a guess as a rule.
