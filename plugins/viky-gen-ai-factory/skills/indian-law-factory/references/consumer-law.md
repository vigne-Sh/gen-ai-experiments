## Landmark cases (verified 2026-10-05)

| Case | Citation | Court / date | Holding | Checked against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New India Assurance Co. Ltd. v. Hilli Multipurpose Cold Storage Pvt. Ltd. | (2020) 5 SCC 757 | Supreme Court, Constitution Bench, 4 March 2020 | Under the 1986 Act s.13(2), the written version must be filed within 30 days, extendable by at most 15 days — **45 days is the hard limit**; consumer fora cannot accept it later | [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/02/22/supreme-court-spells-out-if-consumer-fora-can-apply-the-new-india-assurance-company-ltd-judgment-prospectively-and-accept-the-written-statement-beyond-the-period-of-45-days/) |
| Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha | (1995) 6 SCC 651 (AIR 1996 SC 550) | Supreme Court, 13 November 1995 | Medical services given for payment are a "service" under the Consumer Protection Act, so patients can complain to consumer fora | [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/723973/), [Legal Service India](https://www.legalserviceindia.com/legal/article-6010-case-analysis-vp-shanta-v-s-indian-medical-association.html) |

Caution on *Hilli*: it was decided under the **1986** Act. The 2019 Act re-enacts the 30+15 day scheme in its own section — check the current section number and whether the same hard limit is applied to the 2019 Act before citing it for a live case.
