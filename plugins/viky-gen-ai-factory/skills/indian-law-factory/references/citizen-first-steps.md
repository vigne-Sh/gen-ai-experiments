# Citizen first steps — know your basic rights, with no lawyer and no money

**Last checked:** 2026-10-05

This is legal information to help a person understand where they stand and what to do first. It is not legal advice and not a substitute for a lawyer — but it is written for someone who cannot afford one and doesn't know where to start. Each fact is marked **verified** (checked in a live search on the date above, source named) or **general knowledge** (widely settled, but read the current text before relying on it).

## 1. If a crime has been done to you

| Step | What to know | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Report it at **any** police station | For a cognizable offence the police must register an FIR (BNSS **173**, formerly CrPC 154), and **Zero FIR** means any station must register it even if the crime happened elsewhere, then transfer it. You can also report electronically (e-FIR) — it must be signed by you within three days to be taken on record. | **verified** — [LawSikho](https://lawsikho.com/blog/fir-registration-bnss-vs-crpc-section-173-154-changes/), [BPR&D SOP](https://bprd.nic.in/uploads/pdf/SOP_on_Zero_FIR%20&%20eFIR%20-%20NCL%202023.pdf) |
| Police refusing to register an FIR | The Supreme Court held registration is mandatory when information discloses a cognizable offence — *Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh*, (2014) 2 SCC 1 (judgment of 12 November 2013). Limited exceptions allow a short preliminary enquiry (e.g. matrimonial, commercial, medical-negligence, corruption, long-delay cases). | **verified** — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/10239019/), [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/critical-analysis-of-lalita-kumari-v-govt-of-u-p/) |
| Preliminary enquiry under BNSS | For cognizable offences punishable with 3 years or more but under 7 years, police may — with prior approval of an officer not below DSP — hold a preliminary enquiry, ordinarily within 14 days, before registering. | **verified** — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/supreme-court-judgment-imran-pratapgadhi-vs-state-of-gujarat-explains-preliminary-inquiry-section-173-bnss-287964), [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/08/18/section-173-3-bnss-preliminary-enquiry-lalita-kumari/) |
| Ask for a copy of the FIR | The person reporting is entitled to a free copy of the FIR. | general knowledge |
| Keep your own evidence | Photographs, messages, call records, bills, medical papers, names of witnesses — keep **originals**, and make copies. Electronic evidence needs a certificate under BSA s.63 to be admissible. | s.63 **verified** (see `criminal-law.md`) |

## 2. If you are being arrested or called to the police station

| Right | Detail | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Be told **why** | The arresting officer must tell you the grounds of arrest (BNSS **47**; Article 22(1) of the Constitution). | **verified** — [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/mandatory-disclosure-of-arrest-grounds-article-22-bnss/) |
| Talk to a lawyer of your choice | Article 22(1). If you cannot afford one, you are entitled to a free lawyer (see section 4). | **verified** |
| Be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours | Excluding travel time (BNSS **58**, Article 22(2)). Nobody can be held longer without a magistrate's order. | **verified** — [Pahuja Law Academy](https://www.pahujalawacademy.com/section-58-person-arrested-not-to-be-detained-more-than-twenty-four-hours) |
| Notice instead of arrest for lesser offences | For offences punishable up to 7 years the police should ordinarily issue a **notice to appear** (BNSS **35**, formerly CrPC 41A) rather than arrest, and must record written reasons if they do arrest — *Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar*, (2014) 8 SCC 273. **Always obey the notice and attend** — ignoring it is what makes arrest likely. | **verified** (see `criminal-law.md`) |
| Basic protections in custody | The Supreme Court's **D.K. Basu** guidelines (AIR 1997 SC 610): arresting officers must carry visible name identification; an **arrest memo** signed by a witness and countersigned by you; a relative or friend informed promptly; and a right to consult a lawyer. These are now largely built into BNSS ss.35–47. | **verified** — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/dk-basu-vs-state-of-west-bengal-1997-case-analysis/), [LawLex](https://lawnotes.co/landmark-guidelines-on-arrest-and-detention-d-k-basu-v-state-of-west-bengal-1997/) — SCC volume number is reported inconsistently across sources, so cite the AIR reference |

## 3. Getting out: bail and settlement (what the sections are called now)

| Situation | Section | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Bailable offence — bail is your right | BNSS **478** (CrPC 436) | **verified** |
| Non-bailable offence — court's discretion | BNSS **480** (CrPC 437) | **verified** |
| You fear arrest — ask the court beforehand | Anticipatory bail, BNSS **482** (CrPC 438) | **verified** |
| Police are taking too long to file the chargesheet | **Default bail**, BNSS **187(3)** (CrPC 167(2)) — an automatic right once the time limit passes (reported as 60 or 90 days depending on the offence; not available for offences punishable with death/life as described in the sources — verify the exact limit for your case) | **verified** — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/draft-bail-application-bnss/) |
| Stuck in jail as an undertrial | BNSS **479**: release after serving a fraction of the maximum sentence — one-third for a first-time offender, one-half otherwise — and never beyond the maximum sentence itself | **verified** — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/maximum-detention-of-an-undertrial-and-release-section-479-bnss/) |
| Both sides want to settle | **Compounding**, BNSS **359** (CrPC 320) — only offences the section lists can be settled, some with court permission | **verified** — [ApniLaw](https://www.apnilaw.com/legal-articles/acts/section-320-crpc-vs-section-359-bnss-compounding-of-criminal-offences/) |
| FIR is false or abusive | Quashing, High Court under BNSS **528** | **verified** |

## 4. Free legal help — you do not have to pay for a lawyer

- **Who is entitled:** under Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987, free legal services are available to, among others, **women**, **children** (till 18), members of **SC/ST**, **persons in custody**, and persons whose annual income is below the limit fixed by their state (₹5 lakh for matters in the Supreme Court). The income test does **not** apply to women, children, SC/ST members and persons in custody. **verified** — [NALSA legal-aid page](https://nalsa.gov.in/legal-aid/), [NALSA FAQs](https://nalsa.gov.in/faqs/), [Lawgist](https://lawgist.in/legal-services-authorities-act/12).
- **Where to ask:** the **District Legal Services Authority (DLSA)** in your district court complex, the **Taluk legal services committee**, or the **State Legal Services Authority**; the national helpline is **15100** (listed by NALSA; carried over from an earlier pass — confirm on nalsa.gov.in). If you are arrested, tell the magistrate you want a legal-aid lawyer — they are expected to arrange one.
- **Tamil Nadu:** TNSLSA and the district DLSAs — contact details are in `SKILL.md` and must be re-checked live.
- **Also free:** Lok Adalats (settlement forums) and legal-aid clinics run by the legal services authorities — **general knowledge**, ask the DLSA.

## 5. Being a law-abiding citizen — habits that keep you out of trouble

All general knowledge, listed because they are the most common ways ordinary people end up in a fight they didn't need:
- **Never ignore a notice, summons or court date.** Reply in writing, on time, and keep proof of delivery. Missing it turns a small matter into a warrant.
- **Put important deals in writing.** Money lent, goods sold, property bought — a signed paper, a receipt, a bank transfer reference. Pay by bank, not cash, where you can.
- **Property:** a sale agreement is not ownership — a sale deed of immovable property must be **registered** (see `property-law.md`).
- **Cheques:** a bounced cheque can be a criminal case (NI Act s.138, see `corporate-and-commercial-law.md`) — a legal notice and a short deadline apply, so act quickly if you receive one.
- **Do not sign blank papers or documents you haven't read,** and keep copies of everything you sign.
- **Online:** do not share another person's private images, do not impersonate anyone, and report fake accounts and fraud promptly (IT Act, see `cyber-law.md`; National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal).
- **Driving:** third-party insurance is compulsory; rash driving is an offence (BNS 281).
- **Dealing with police:** stay calm, don't resist or obstruct (BNS 221), note names and badge numbers, never pay anyone to "settle" an FIR, and never help create or destroy evidence (BNS 238).
- **Know the clock.** Civil claims (money, property, contracts) must be filed within the limitation period under the **Limitation Act 1963** — check the period before waiting.

## 6. What this skill will not do

It will not tell you to lie, delay, hide or destroy evidence, avoid a summons, or pressure a witness — those turn a defensible position into a crime. The lawful ways to defend are: reply to the notice, cooperate with the investigation, seek bail or anticipatory bail, ask the court to quash an abusive FIR, settle where the law allows, and keep honest records.
