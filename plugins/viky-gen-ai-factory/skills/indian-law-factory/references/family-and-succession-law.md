### Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (checked 2026-10-13)

A **civil** remedy before a Magistrate — the woman does not have to file
a criminal case to use it.

| Section | Order the Magistrate can make |
|---|---|
| s.18 | Protection order — stop the violence, contact, or entering her workplace |
| s.19 | Residence order — she cannot be thrown out of the shared household |
| s.20 | Monetary relief — expenses and losses, including maintenance |
| s.21 | Temporary custody of children |
| s.22 | Compensation, including for mental torture and emotional distress |
| s.26 | These reliefs can also be asked for in other cases (divorce, maintenance) in a civil or family court |

Checked against: [Act text, National Commission for Women](https://cdn.ncw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/TheProtectionofWomenfromDomesticViolenceAct2005_0.pdf), [PRS India](https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2005/the-protection-of-women-from-domestic-violence-act-2005.pdf). Who to approach: the Protection Officer (District Social Welfare Office) or the Magistrate directly.

### Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961, as amended (checked 2026-10-13)

| Section | Offence | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| s.3 | Giving or taking dowry | At least **5 years** and a fine of at least **₹15,000 or the value of the dowry**, whichever is more (less only for special reasons recorded by the court) |
| s.4 | Demanding dowry | **6 months to 2 years** and a fine up to **₹10,000** |

Some states amended these sections — check the state version. Checked
against: [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/laws-prohibiting-dowry-india/), [LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/dowry-prohibition-act-1961/2540), [India Code PDF](https://indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/16243/1/196128.pdf).

## Succession — Hindu Succession Act, 1956, as amended 2005

| Claim | Checked against |
|---|---|
| The Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 rewrote Section 6: a daughter becomes a coparcener in a Mitakshara joint Hindu family **by birth**, in the same manner as a son, with the same rights, liabilities, and right to seek partition — regardless of her marital status | [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/critical-analysis-hindu-succession-amendment-act-2005/), [Indian Kanoon - Section 6](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1883337/) |

**Wills (checked 2026-10-13):** under Indian Succession Act s.63(c) an
ordinary will must be **attested by at least two witnesses**, who sign in
the testator's presence; a will with one witness fails (Supreme Court,
2019). To prove it in court, at least one attesting witness is usually
called. Checked against: [Supreme Court judgment, 17 Dec 2019](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2018/23443/23443_2018_14_27_19233_Judgement_17-Dec-2019.pdf), [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/section-68-evidence-act-at-least-one-attesting-witness-is-required-to-prove-the-execution-of-a-will-for-it-to-be-valid-under-section-63-of-the-succession-act-sc-reiterates-1498474).

Not independently verified in this pass: which parts of the Indian
Succession Act apply to Muslims, intestate succession for non-Hindus,
whether probate is needed in a given state, and Muslim personal law on
inheritance (including the one-third limit on bequests) — research live, this is an area
where the applicable law depends heavily on the parties' personal law.

## Landmark: daughters' coparcenary right (verified 2026-10-05)

| Case | Citation | Holding | Checked against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma | (2020) 9 SCC 1 | A daughter is a coparcener **by birth**; her right does not depend on her father having been alive on 9 September 2005 (the date of the amendment), and applies to daughters born before or after it | [Record of Law](https://recordoflaw.in/vineeta-sharma-vs-rakesh-sharma-2020-9-scc-1/), [ITAT Online digest](https://itatonline.org/digest/vineeta-sharma-v-rakesh-sharma-ors-sc-www-itatonline-org-manu-sc-0582-2020/) |
