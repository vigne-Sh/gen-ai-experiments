# BNS ↔ IPC — the offences ordinary people actually meet

**Last checked:** 2026-10-05 (kidnapping row corrected and ~12 sections added the same day)

Read this first:
- **IPC (1860) had 511 sections; BNS (2023) has 358.** BNS has applied to offences committed **on or after 1 July 2024**. An offence committed **before** that date is still dealt with under the IPC (and CrPC procedure rules as saved by BNSS) — you cannot be punished under a harsher new section for an old act (Article 20(1) of the Constitution). Always ask "on what date did it happen?" first.
- **This is not all 511 sections.** It is the ~70 sections a citizen is most likely to come across. Every mapping below was confirmed against at least one secondary legal source in a live search (sources at the bottom). The authoritative full table — the official BNS–IPC corresponding-section table — could not be reached from the sandbox this was built in (the host was blocked), so for any section not listed here, **look it up live** on [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/) or the official comparison table, and never guess a number.
- **"What must be shown"** is a plain-language summary of the main ingredients, written from general legal knowledge, **not** quoted statute text. The real section has exceptions and provisos — read the bare text before relying on it. Punishments are deliberately not listed (they were changed for several offences; look them up live).

## Property and money offences

| Offence | IPC | BNS | What must be shown (summary) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theft | 378 / 379 | 303 | Dishonestly taking movable property out of someone's possession without their consent |
| Extortion | 383 / 384 | 308 | Intentionally putting someone in fear of injury and dishonestly making them hand over property |
| Robbery | 390 / 392 | 309 | Theft or extortion done with violence or fear of instant hurt |
| Dacoity | 391 / 395 | 310 | Robbery by five or more persons together |
| Criminal breach of trust | 405 / 406 | 316 | Property **lawfully entrusted** to you, then dishonestly misused or kept |
| Receiving stolen property | 411 | 317(2) | Dishonestly receiving/retaining property knowing or believing it is stolen |
| Cheating | 415 / 420 | 318 (420 → 318(4)) | Deceiving someone so they hand over property/consent to something — **dishonest intention at the very start** is the key |
| Forgery | 463 / 465 | 336 | Making a false document or electronic record intending to cause damage, support a false claim, or commit fraud |
| Forgery for cheating / of valuable security or will | 468 / 467 | 336(3) / 338 | Forgery done to cheat; forgery of valuable security, will, etc. (both reported cognizable and non-bailable) |
| Mischief | 425, 426 | 324 | Intending/knowing to cause wrongful loss or damage to property and doing so |
| Criminal trespass / house-trespass | 441, 448 | 329 | Entering property with intent to commit an offence or to intimidate/insult/annoy |

> **Cheating vs criminal breach of trust are opposites on the same facts.** Cheating needs a dishonest intention from the very beginning; criminal breach of trust starts with a *lawful* handover that is later misused. The Supreme Court has held the two cannot stand together on the same allegations ([LiveLaw report](https://www.livelaw.in/supreme-court/offences-of-cheating-criminal-breach-of-trust-cannot-co-exist-on-same-allegations-supreme-court-305118)) — a common point in defending a business/money dispute that has been dressed up as a criminal case.

## New in the BNS, and what was dropped (checked in live search)

**New offences with no IPC equivalent (verify the text and punishment live before relying):**
- **Organised crime** — BNS 111; **petty organised crime** — BNS 112 (syndicated unlawful activity such as kidnapping, extortion, land-grabbing, contract killing, economic and cyber crime).
- **Terrorist act** — BNS 113 (earlier handled mainly under special laws such as UAPA, which continues).
- **Mob lynching** — BNS 103(2): murder (or grievous hurt) by five or more persons acting together on a ground such as race, caste, sex, language or personal belief; reported punishment for murder is life imprisonment or death.
- **Hit and run** — BNS 106(2): death by rash/negligent driving where the driver flees without reporting to the police or a magistrate — reported up to 10 years and fine; if the driver reports promptly, BNS 106(1) applies (up to 5 years).

**Dropped or changed:**
- IPC 377 (unnatural offences) — omitted (read down by the Supreme Court earlier).
- IPC 497 (adultery) — omitted (struck down by the Supreme Court).
- IPC 309 (attempt to commit suicide) — omitted, except a narrow revival in BNS 226 for suicide attempts intended to compel or restrain a public servant.
- IPC 124A (sedition) — replaced by BNS 152 (a recast offence, not a renumbering).
